## Summary

Between corn silos and interstate glow, a handful of drivers, a night-shift nurse, and one very awake dog all notice the same wrong star. It holds still. It skips. It paints a soft teal that doesn’t belong on any constellation chart.

Phones come out. Footage is blurry in the traditional way. Local news runs a segment titled something like “Mystery Lights Over County Road.” Comments section achieves lift-off.

## Incident narrative (composite)

**02:41 UTC** — Object observed low on the western horizon, stationary for ~90 seconds.

**02:43 UTC** — Lateral “skip” of several degrees with no continuous trail. Witnesses disagree on distance by a factor of approximately “a lot.”

**02:46 UTC** — Soft pulse. Teal to white. Then nothing. Stars resume their union-mandated twinkling.

A composite witness (“Mira K.”) later told our fictional intake desk:

> “It didn’t feel like a plane. It felt like the sky had a cursor and someone dragged it.”
