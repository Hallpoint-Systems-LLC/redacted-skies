## What the researchers concluded

Astronomers report the **first direct radio emission** from an exoplanet: **Beta Pictoris b** (~**63 light-years**, ~**12 Jupiter masses**). **Edo Berger** (Harvard / CfA) and lead author **Kevin Ortiz Ceballos** describe **repeating bursts** as **auroral radio emission** driven by a magnetic field at least **200 times stronger than Jupiter’s** — planetary magnetism, not artificial transmitters.

Key points from the reporting:

- Instrument: **MeerKAT** (64 dishes, South Africa).
- Interpretation: magnetic aurorae / extreme planetary field — **not** intelligent life.
- Paper posted to **arXiv September 15**; **awaiting peer review**.
- Independent comments from **Joseph Callingham** (Amsterdam) and **Jonathan Nichols** (Leicester) urge **caution until peer review**.
- System context: Beta Pictoris is ~**23 million years** old; planet **b** discovered **2008**; planets **c** (**2019**) and **d** (**2026**).
